**KindPath Collective Inc**

# Incident Management Policy (including Reportable Incidents)

Version 1.0 | Effective: 29 August 2026 | Review: 29 August 2027 | Owner: Director

## 1. Purpose

To ensure every incident affecting a participant’s health, safety or wellbeing — and every incident affecting a worker in the course of delivering support — is recorded, responded to, and learned from, and that reportable incidents reach the NDIS Commission when they must.

## 2. Scope

All KindPath staff, contractors, volunteers and family-member workers, and all participants and workers involved in a KindPath-delivered support.

## 3. Legislative and standards basis

* NDIS Practice Standards, Module 1: Rights and Responsibilities — Incident management.
* NDIS (Provider Registration and Practice Standards) Rules 2018, Part 2 — reportable incidents.
* Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (NSW) — notifiable incidents involving workers.

## 4. What counts as an incident

An incident is any event that has caused, or could have caused, harm to a participant or worker in connection with a KindPath support — including near misses. This deliberately includes lower-severity events (a missed medication reminder, a fall with no injury, a professional-boundary lapse, a vehicle near-miss during transport) as well as serious ones. Recording the small ones is what lets patterns be caught before something serious happens.

**Reportable incidents** (once KindPath is a registered NDIS provider) are a legally defined subset that must be notified to the NDIS Commission: - the death of a participant; - serious injury of a participant; - abuse or neglect of a participant; - unlawful sexual or physical contact with, or assault of, a participant; - sexual misconduct committed against, or in the presence of, a participant; - the use of an unauthorised restrictive practice.

## 5. Procedures

1. **Immediate response.** Safety first — first aid, emergency services (000) if needed, remove ongoing risk.
2. **Notify the Director** the same day for any incident, immediately for anything serious or reportable.
3. **Record in the Incident Register** within 24 hours: date, time, people involved, what happened (factual, observed, no speculation), immediate action taken, and follow-up needed.
4. **Reportable incident notification.** Once registered, notify the NDIS Commission within 24 hours of becoming aware of a reportable incident (initial notification), with a full report within 5 business days. Until KindPath is registered, treat the same categories as an internal trigger for immediate Director escalation and, where relevant, independent legal advice and/or a police report — registration status does not remove the underlying duty of care.
5. **Debrief and learning.** Every incident is reviewed for what changes to practice, supervision, training, or the physical/support environment would reduce recurrence. This feeds the Quality Management and Continuous Improvement Policy.
6. **Worker incidents.** An incident that harms or endangers a worker (including a psychosocial harm — for example, an unsafe or distressing interaction) is recorded and responded to with the same seriousness as a participant incident, and is separately relevant under WHS obligations.

## 6. Roles and responsibilities

* **Director:** primary point of escalation, responsible for Commission notification once registered, and for closing the loop on every logged incident.
* **Workers:** report every incident, including near-misses and ones that reflect on their own practice.

## 8. Review
