**KindPath Collective Inc**

# Staff and Volunteer Code of Conduct

Version 1.0 | Effective: 29 August 2026 | Review: 29 August 2027 | Owner: Director

## 1. Purpose

To set KindPath’s own internal behavioural expectations for anyone working or volunteering on its behalf — employees, contractors, volunteers, and family members providing support — building on, and going further than, the minimum set by the NDIS Code of Conduct.

## 2. Scope

Everyone delivering any KindPath support or representing KindPath in any capacity.

## 3. Expected conduct

* Treat every participant, colleague, and member of the public with respect, regardless of how tired, frustrated, or under-resourced you feel.
* Maintain clear professional boundaries in every support relationship, including — especially — where a personal or family relationship already exists with the participant. If you’re ever unsure whether something (a gift, a hug, a favour, contact outside agreed hours) is appropriate, ask before doing it, not after.
* Be honest about your own limits. If a support situation is beyond your training, comfort, or capacity, say so — to the Director, and where safe, to the participant.
* Do not use your position for personal, financial or social advantage beyond what’s disclosed and agreed under the Conflict of Interest Policy.
* Follow this folio’s policies in practice, not just in principle — and say so when a policy doesn’t match reality, so it can be fixed.
* Represent KindPath’s values (as reflected in its founding doctrine documents) without overstating KindPath’s current capability, registration status, or resources to a participant or funder.

## 4. Specific note on family-member and dual-relationship workers

Where a worker has an existing personal relationship with a participant (as is currently the case), the ordinary “stranger boundary” instincts that structure most disability support relationships don’t automatically apply, and have to be built deliberately instead. This means: - Physical affection (hugs, for example) that might feel natural in a personal friendship is treated as a workplace boundary question in the support relationship, discussed explicitly and agreed (or declined) in advance rather than decided in the moment. - A participant’s request that blurs the line between “friend” and “supported person” (asking for a hug, asking for help outside agreed hours, sharing something that sounds like it needs a different kind of support) is met kindly, but the worker checks with the Director about how to handle it rather than improvising, especially early in a support relationship or where a positive behaviour support plan doesn’t yet exist to guide it. - Getting this wrong occasionally, and correcting it — as already happened and was handled appropriately — is normal and expected in an emerging organisation without formalised training yet in place. What matters is that it’s named, discussed, and corrected, not left unaddressed.

## 5. Breach

Breaches are addressed through supervision in the first instance for lower-level or first-time issues, and through the Safeguarding Policy for anything involving participant safety.

## 6. Acknowledgement

Every worker signs this Code at induction and annually thereafter.
