**KindPath Collective Inc**

# Service and Client Matching Policy

Version 1.0 | Effective: 7 September 2026 | Review: 7 September 2027 | Owner: Director

## 1. Purpose

To decide, openly and consistently, whether KindPath is the right match for a prospective participant's support needs right now — and to make that decision on the same grounds we'd be comfortable defending in public, not on any basis we'd only admit to privately.

## 2. Legislative and standards basis

NDIS Practice Standards, Core Module: Provision of Supports — Access to supports. NDIS Code of Conduct point 3 (supports delivered safely, competently, and within scope). Disability Discrimination Act 1992 (Cth).

## 3. Policy statement

KindPath keeps one version of the truth about how it decides who it supports — there is no internal criteria that differs from what's published here. If a matching decision isn't something we'd say to the person's face and stand behind publicly, it isn't a valid reason.

Matching is decided on **capacity and safe scope of practice**, not on how straightforward, low-risk, or likely-to-succeed a person's situation is. KindPath does not screen out prospective participants because their needs are complex, their goals are hard to achieve, or supporting them would be difficult — those are often exactly the people advocacy exists for. What KindPath does screen for is whether it can currently deliver support safely and competently, given that KindPath is a small, currently-unregistered sole-trader practice, not a large multidisciplinary provider.

## 4. Matching criteria

A referral is a match when, at the time of intake, KindPath can honestly say yes to all of:

1. **Capacity.** KindPath has an available worker with time to take this on without compromising the quality of support already committed to existing participants.
2. **Safe scope of practice.** The support needed doesn't require registered-provider capability, clinical/specialist qualifications, or formal multi-worker coordination that KindPath doesn't currently have (for example: specialist behaviour support involving restrictive practices, complex clinical/health-care needs requiring registered nursing, or support requiring rostering across many workers at once).
3. **Reach.** KindPath can actually get to the person — in person on Bundjalung Country / Northern NSW, or remotely where that suits the support type and the participant.
4. **No unmanaged conflict of interest.** Where a referral involves a personal or family connection to a KindPath worker (see the Conflict of Interest Policy — currently a live, disclosed situation, not a hypothetical), the match decision is not made by that worker alone.

None of the following are matching criteria, and never will be: disability type or diagnosis, complexity or severity of need, likely NDIS outcome, how much funding the person has, or how easy the person is to work with.

## 5. Procedure

1. **Initial contact.** Whoever makes contact (participant, family, support coordinator, self-referral) is asked what support they're looking for, in plain language, before anything else.
2. **Matching conversation.** The Director checks the four criteria above against what's known so far. Where a personal/family conflict of interest is present, the Conflict of Interest Policy's independent-input requirement applies before a decision is finalised.
3. **Decision, given honestly.** One of three outcomes, told to the person directly and in plain language:
    - **Yes, and we have capacity now** → proceed to intake under the Support Planning and Service Agreements Policy.
    - **Yes, but we're at capacity** → the person is told this plainly, given a realistic estimate of when capacity might open, and offered other options (support coordinator, NDIS Provider Finder) rather than being left waiting with no information.
    - **Not a safe or appropriate match right now** → the specific reason is given honestly (not a vague "not a good fit"), and KindPath helps with a warm handover to a more suitable provider where possible, consistent with the Transitions (Entry and Exit) Policy's approach to handovers.
4. **Documented.** Every matching decision (yes, waitlisted, or no) is logged with its reason, so patterns — including any drift toward avoiding complex referrals — can be reviewed under Quality Management and Continuous Improvement.

## 6. Roles and responsibilities

* **Director:** makes and documents matching decisions; seeks independent input whenever a personal or family conflict of interest is present, per the Conflict of Interest Policy; reviews logged decisions periodically for any pattern of avoiding complex or high-need referrals.
